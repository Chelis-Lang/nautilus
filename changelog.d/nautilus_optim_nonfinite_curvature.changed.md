`Nautilus.Optim.newton_minimize_1d` now certifies the point it stops on in every case, where before two of its three exits returned a point no curvature check had looked at. The check lived only in the converged branch. `opt_nmin_rec` also exits at `eq(x_next, x)` -- a **stall**, where the Newton step rounded away to nothing -- and returned `x` from there directly, from a branch it had entered because `|df(x)| >= tol`. And the converged branch was unreachable at some tolerances: `lt(ag, tol)` is false for every gradient when `tol` is zero, negative or NaN, so at those tolerances the stall was the *only* exit. Nine wrong answers followed, all measured at the `=0.18.12` pin on `(x-3)^2` with the correct first derivative, `tol = 1e-6` and `max_iters = 100` unless stated. From the stall with a non-stationary gradient: a constant `ddf` of `+inf` or `-inf` at `x0 = 5.0` returned `5.0` (unchanged at `max_iters = 10000`, because `step = g / +-inf` is `+-0`); a constant `-1e30` returned `5.0`; a `ddf = -2.0` inconsistent with `df` diverged and returned `inf` at `max_iters = 1000`; an everywhere-`+inf` first derivative with a correct `ddf = 2.0` returned `-inf`; and a unit first derivative with `ddf = 2.0` from `x0 = +-inf` returned `+-inf`. From the stall at `tol <= 0`, where nothing else could fire: a `-inf` curvature at the minimiser returned `3.0`, one that becomes `-inf` only at the point reached returned `3.0`, a curvature of `0.002` returned `3.0` against a `0.01` floor, and Newton on the concave `-(x-3)^2` with its own correct derivatives returned `3.0`, which is the **maximiser**. Two changes fix all nine. A stalled point is certified by the same `opt_certify_min_f32` the converged branch uses, and a gradient of exactly zero now counts as converged at any tolerance, which is what makes the converged branch reachable at `tol <= 0` and a NaN `tol`. The stall carries one extra requirement that convergence does not, and the asymmetry is deliberate: the point and its curvature must both be finite, because a stall has satisfied no stationarity test. **An infinite curvature at a converged point is therefore still accepted, and that is load-bearing rather than an oversight.** `+inf` is strictly positive and above `0.01`, an infinite `ddf` is reachable by overflow inside a *correct* expression, and rejecting it loses real answers: a `ddf = +inf` with the consistent `df(x) = 2e30*(x-3)` at `x0 = 3.0`, and a `ddf` finite where the steps are taken and `+inf` only at the point reached, both return the true minimiser. An intermediate revision of this change rejected a non-finite curvature at both sites and lost exactly those, at `tol > 0`; a later one fixed that at `tol > 0` only and still lost them at `tol <= 0` and NaN `tol`, six rows in all. Both were caught by review, not by reasoning, and the second is why a zero gradient now converges on its own. A reviewer proposed relaxing the stall's finiteness leg to `or(eq(g, 0.0))` instead; that was implemented and measured identical on all 37 probe rows, and the convergence-test change was preferred because it repairs the predicate that was unable to express convergence rather than compensating for it at the other exit. Each leg was measured for its own coverage by mutation, with the anchor match asserted after two earlier mutations silently no-opped on formatter-rewrapped text: the curvature leg alone owns the `+-inf` rows, the iterate leg alone owns the `x0 = +-inf` rows, the certification call owns `-1e30` and the concave row, and a third leg on the gradient was dropped after it changed no row -- at a stall with a finite point and a finite curvature the gradient cannot be non-finite. **One case is lost against the previous behaviour, and the loss is forced rather than chosen.** A stall is rejected when its curvature is infinite and accepted when it is merely huge, so one ulp above the minimiser of `(x-3)^2` with `ddf = +inf` and `tol = 0.0` returns NaN where `origin/main` returns `3.0000002` -- the gradient there is `4.8e-7`, not zero, so no convergence test fires, and the step underflows. A finite `ddf = 1e30` at the same point and tolerance still returns it. Finiteness is the only discriminator the method has at a stall, and both alternatives were implemented and run against the suite rather than argued: rejecting every stall whose gradient is not exactly zero fails `test_newton_quartic_at_sqrt2`, a genuine convergence that stalls one ulp from `sqrt(2)` with a gradient of `5.6e-7`, and accepting every stall fails the three tests that pin nautilus#117 itself. The middle is therefore what the pre-existing corpus and the issue jointly allow, not a preference. Any `tol` above the gradient accepts the point through the convergence test, which is the remedy and is pinned beside the loss. This is the third round in which review found this same class -- a correct answer at a near-stationary point with an infinite curvature -- so rather than patch again the limit is stated in `docs/book/src/solvers/optim.md`, `docs/book/src/appendix/limitations.md` and `spec/scope.md`, and pinned by a test that asserts all three outcomes together. **One further stall the change still accepts, stated as a precondition.** A constant `ddf = 1e30` against the correct `df` underflows the step at `x0 = 5.0`, stalls with everything finite, and certifies on that large positive value, returning `5.0`. That pair is not a consistent `df`/`ddf`, and no single-point evaluation distinguishes it from a genuinely sharp minimum, so it is recorded as a caller precondition in `docs/book/src/appendix/limitations.md` and `spec/scope.md` rather than as a defect; a test pins the accepted answer so that changing the disposition must change a test. Twelve tests cover the change. **Six are red against `origin/main`** and the mutation attribution above says which guard each needs. **Six are green against `origin/main` by design**, pinning what must not move: the `+inf`-at-a-stationary-point rows, the same rows across `tol = 0`, NaN, `-1.0` and `-inf`, a stall at an exactly zero gradient still certifying, the finite-curvature cases including the `0.01` floor and the `1e-30` step guard, a `tol = 10.0` call that accepts `x0 = 5.0` on its own gradient rather than by stalling, the `1e30` precondition, and the forced asymmetry above with its positive-`tol` remedy. One of those six is red against the superseded intermediate commit rather than against `origin/main`, which is the regression test for the lost-answer defect review found.
