`Nautilus.Optim.newton_minimize_1d` no longer returns a point it has not certified when the Newton step stops moving the iterate. `opt_nmin_rec` reaches `x_next == x` from the branch it entered *because* `|df(x)| >= tol`, and returned `x` from there without any curvature check at all; the check lives only in the converged branch. That stall is reachable several ways and every one of them produced a plain float, measured at the `=0.18.12` pin on `(x-3)^2` with the correct first derivative: a constant `ddf` of `+inf` or `-inf` at `x0 = 5.0` returned `5.0`, unchanged at `max_iters = 10000`, because `step = g / +-inf` is `+-0`; a constant `ddf = -1e30` returned `5.0` the same way; a `ddf = -2.0` inconsistent with `df` diverged and returned `inf` once the budget allowed it to overflow (`max_iters = 1000`); an everywhere-`+inf` first derivative with a correct `ddf = 2.0` returned `-inf`; and Newton on the concave `-(x-3)^2` with its own correct derivatives at `tol = 0.0` returned `3.0`, which is the *maximiser*. A stalled point is now accepted only on the same terms as a converged one: the point and its curvature must be finite, and the curvature must clear the existing `0.01` floor and sign test. Those seven measured rows become NaN and no other row moves. The stall remains an acceptance path rather than a rejection, because it has to be: `x - df(x)/ddf(x)` rounds back to `x` at the limit of f32 resolution with `|df(x)|` still above `tol`, which is how `tol = 0` and a NaN `tol` converge at all, and `tests/optim.ch`'s quartic case `(x^2-2)^2` from `x0 = 1.2` at `tol = 1e-7` stalls one ulp from `sqrt(2)` with a gradient of about `5.6e-7`. An earlier attempt to require an exactly zero gradient at the stall turned that test red, which is how the requirement was found to be wrong. Two legs of the finiteness test were measured for their own coverage before being kept: the curvature leg alone owns the `+-inf` rows, and the iterate leg alone owns a stall at an infinite `x` whose gradient and curvature are both finite and certifying (a unit `df` with `ddf = 2.0` from `x0 = +-inf`, which returned `+-inf`), so neither is redundant; a third leg on the gradient was dropped after a mutation showed it changed no row, since a stall with a finite point and a finite curvature cannot have a non-finite gradient. **A non-finite second derivative at the point the method converges to is deliberately still accepted when it is positive.** An earlier revision of this change rejected it at the convergence site too, and that was wrong in both directions: `+inf` is strictly positive curvature at a point whose stationarity the gradient test has already established, and rejecting it lost correct answers -- `ddf = +inf` with the consistent `df(x) = 2e30*(x-3)` at `x0 = 3.0`, and a `ddf` that is finite where the steps are taken and `+inf` only at the point reached, both returned the true minimiser on `origin/main` and NaN under that revision. The converged branch therefore keeps its NaN-only guard, and `-inf` is rejected there by the pre-existing `lte(h, 0.0)` rather than by anything added here. The two acceptance sites now share one `opt_certify_min_f32` so they cannot drift apart; that shared helper is the whole of the behaviour change at the converged site. **One stall the change deliberately still accepts, now stated as a precondition rather than left silent.** A constant `ddf = 1e30` against the correct `df` underflows the step at `x0 = 5.0`, stalls, and passes the curvature check on a large positive value, returning `5.0`. That pair is not a consistent `df`/`ddf`, and no single-point evaluation distinguishes it from a genuinely sharp minimum -- detecting it needs a consistency test between two caller-supplied functions, which is a different mechanism from anything here. It is therefore recorded as a precondition on the caller in `docs/book/src/appendix/limitations.md` and `spec/scope.md`, not as a second instance of the defect this change fixes: the cases fixed here are ones where a *correct* `ddf` expression can produce a non-finite value by ordinary overflow, or the iterate itself leaves the finite range, and both are detectable from one point. A test pins the accepted answer so that changing that disposition must change a test. Nine tests cover the change. Seven are red against `origin/main` and the per-leg mutation matrix above attributes each to the guard that owns it; two are controls that are green against `origin/main` by design and exist to pin what this change must *not* alter -- the `+inf`-at-convergence rows above, and the finite-curvature cases including `tol = 1e-6`, the `0.01` floor, the `1e-30` step guard, and a `tol = 10.0` call that accepts `x0 = 5.0` on its own gradient, which is the caller's choice of tolerance and not a stall. `newton_minimize_1d` is `alpha` in `dist/stability.json`, so no stability freeze applies, and its signature is unchanged. Addresses nautilus#117, whose body argued from a constant `ddf = -2.0` at `x0 = 5.0` returning NaN that a finite negative curvature is rejected correctly; that call never reaches the curvature check, its NaN came from the `lte(iters, 0)` budget exit, and raising the budget to 1000 returns `inf`. Reaching the sign test needs a stationary point of a concave function, which is now a test.
