`Nautilus.Optim.newton_minimize_1d` now certifies the point it stops on in every case, where before two of its three exits returned a point no curvature check had looked at. The check lived only in the converged branch. `opt_nmin_rec` also exits at `eq(x_next, x)` -- a **stall**, where the Newton step rounded away to nothing -- and returned `x` from there directly, from a branch it had entered because `|df(x)| >= tol`. And the converged branch was unreachable at some tolerances: `lt(ag, tol)` is false for every gradient when `tol` is zero, negative or NaN, so at those tolerances the stall was the *only* exit. Every case below returned a number that is not a minimiser -- no total is given because the rows that hold at any tolerance do not have a well-defined count -- all measured at the `=0.18.12` pin on `(x-3)^2` with the correct first derivative, `tol = 1e-6` and `max_iters = 100` unless stated. From the stall with a non-stationary gradient: a constant `ddf` of `+inf` or `-inf` at `x0 = 5.0` returned `5.0` (unchanged at `max_iters = 10000`, because `step = g / +-inf` is `+-0`); a constant `-1e30` returned `5.0`; a `ddf = -2.0` inconsistent with `df` diverged and returned `inf` at `max_iters = 1000`; an everywhere-`+inf` first derivative with a correct `ddf = 2.0` returned `-inf`; and a unit first derivative with `ddf = 2.0` from `x0 = +-inf` returned `+-inf`. From the stall at `tol <= 0`, where nothing else could fire: a unit `df` from `x0 = +-inf` returned `+-inf`, a `df` that is zero everywhere did the same at any tolerance, and Newton on the concave `-(x-3)^2` with its own correct derivatives returned `3.0`, which is the **maximiser**. Three further `tol <= 0` rows are *not* in that count and are reclassified below, because the number they returned was the correct minimiser and only its certification was missing: a `-inf` curvature at the minimiser, one that becomes `-inf` only at the point reached, and a curvature of `0.002` against the `0.01` floor. An earlier draft counted all of these as wrong answers; they are losses, not fixes. Two changes fix all nine. A stalled point is certified by the same `opt_certify_min_f32` the converged branch uses, and a gradient of exactly zero now counts as converged at any tolerance, which is what makes the converged branch reachable at `tol <= 0` and a NaN `tol`. The stall carries one extra requirement that convergence does not, and the asymmetry is deliberate: the point and its curvature must both be finite, because a stall has satisfied no stationarity test. **An infinite curvature at a converged point is therefore still accepted, and that is load-bearing rather than an oversight.** `+inf` is strictly positive and above `0.01`, an infinite `ddf` is reachable by overflow inside a *correct* expression, and rejecting it loses real answers: a `ddf = +inf` with the consistent `df(x) = 2e30*(x-3)` at `x0 = 3.0`, and a `ddf` finite where the steps are taken and `+inf` only at the point reached, both return the true minimiser. An intermediate revision of this change rejected a non-finite curvature at both sites and lost exactly those, at `tol > 0`; a later one fixed that at `tol > 0` only and still lost them at `tol <= 0` and NaN `tol`, six rows in all. Both were caught by review, not by reasoning, and the second is why a zero gradient now converges on its own. A reviewer proposed relaxing the stall's finiteness leg to `or(eq(g, 0.0))` instead; that was implemented and measured identical on the 37 probe rows then in hand, and the convergence-test change was preferred because it repairs the predicate that was unable to express convergence rather than compensating for it at the other exit. The two are **not** equivalent in general, and a later round found the row where they diverge: the reviewer's form keeps a finiteness test on the stall path, while routing a zero gradient to the converged branch exposed that branch's lack of one, so a gradient that is zero everywhere made `x0 = +-inf` certify and return `+-inf`. That is fixed by moving the point's finiteness requirement into the shared certification, where it now applies at both exits; the stall's own extra requirement is a finite curvature and nothing more. The `+-inf` row was wrong on `origin/main` too, at any tolerance, so this is an additional fix rather than a repair of something this change introduced. Each leg was measured for its own coverage by mutation, with the anchor match asserted after two earlier mutations silently no-opped on formatter-rewrapped text: the curvature leg alone owns the `+-inf` rows, the iterate leg alone owns the `x0 = +-inf` rows, the certification call owns `-1e30` and the concave row, and a third leg on the gradient was dropped after it changed no row -- at a stall with a finite point and a finite curvature the gradient cannot be non-finite. **A family of correct answers is lost against the previous behaviour, and the loss is forced rather than chosen.** A stall is rejected when its curvature is infinite and accepted when it is merely huge, so a point near a minimiser whose `ddf` has overflowed returns NaN where `origin/main` returned the point. Three measured families: one ulp above the minimiser of `(x-3)^2` with `ddf = +inf` at `tol = 0.0`, where the gradient is `4.8e-7` so no convergence test fires; the same at `tol = 1e-9` and at the smallest positive subnormal `tol`; and `2e38*(x-3)^2` with **both derivatives written correctly from that objective**, whose `ddf = 4e38` overflows f32 to `+inf` while its `df = 9.5e31` there stays finite, which returns NaN at `tol = 1e-6` and at `tol = 1.0` alike. A finite `ddf = 1e30` at the same points returns `3.0000002`, and a `-inf` curvature is unrecoverable at any tolerance because the sign test rejects it at the converged exit too. **A second, disjoint family is lost the same way and was missing from an earlier draft of this fragment: a curvature below the `0.01` floor at a stall.** `3.1e-4*(x^2-2)^2`, both derivatives written correctly from it, stalls at `sqrt(2.0f32)` with curvature `0.00496` and gradient `-2.09e-10`, so `tol = 1e-10` and `tol = 0.0` return NaN where they returned `1.4142135`; `tol = 1e-6` returned NaN before and after, because the converged exit was already applying the floor. No tolerance recovers it, so the bound stated for the overflow family does not cover this one. This is the documented flat-minimum rejection applied uniformly rather than a new rule, and it is why the earlier `0.002` row belongs here rather than among the fixes. A mutation dropping the floor from the certification showed it had **zero** stall-exit test coverage before this family was pinned, which is the axis that hid it: the stall curvature's magnitude under a consistent objective. The escape is a `tol` above `|df(x)|` at the stalled point, which routes it through the convergence test -- but whether one exists depends on the objective's scale, and for the `2e38` family it does not. An earlier draft of this fragment and of the docs claimed a positive tolerance was the remedy; that was false for exactly this family and is corrected rather than restated. Finiteness is the only discriminator the method has at a stall, and both alternatives were implemented and measured against the suite rather than argued: `settled := eq(ag, 0.0)` -- reject every stall that is not exactly stationary -- fails **three** tests, of which only `test_newton_quartic_at_sqrt2` is pre-existing (a genuine convergence that stalls exactly *at* `sqrt(2.0f32)` from `x0 = 1.2`, with a gradient of `-6.74e-7` and a step of `-4.2e-8`); `settled := true` -- accept every stall -- also fails **three**, including the test pinning nautilus#117's own headline case. So the forcing rests on the quartic plus the issue, not on a broad corpus, and the other blockers in that count are tests this change authored. This is the third review round to find this same class -- a correct answer at a near-stationary point with an overflowing curvature -- so rather than patch a fourth time the limit is stated in `docs/book/src/solvers/optim.md`, `docs/book/src/appendix/limitations.md` and `spec/scope.md`, and pinned by two tests that assert the loss, its bound, and the case where the bound is unreachable. **One further stall the change still accepts, stated as a precondition.** A constant `ddf = 1e30` against the correct `df` underflows the step at `x0 = 5.0`, stalls with everything finite, and certifies on that large positive value, returning `5.0`. That pair is not a consistent `df`/`ddf`, and no single-point evaluation distinguishes it from a genuinely sharp minimum, so it is recorded as a caller precondition in `docs/book/src/appendix/limitations.md` and `spec/scope.md` rather than as a defect; a test pins the accepted answer so that changing the disposition must change a test.
