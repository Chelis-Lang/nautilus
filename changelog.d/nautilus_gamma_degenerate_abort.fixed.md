`Nautilus.Distributions.gamma_cdf` and `gamma_sf` validated neither parameter, so a zero or negative `scale` produced a non-finite standardised argument `x / scale`, the continued fraction never satisfied its convergence test, and the recursion spent its whole 200-iteration budget. `chelis eval --file` evaluates on the `main` thread and overflows its stack at about 135 frames of a body that size, so the process died with `fatal runtime error: stack overflow` and exit 134 and no diagnostic a Chelis caller could catch; `chi_squared_cdf`, `chi_squared_sf`, `Nautilus.Testing.chi_squared_p_value`, `gamma_inv_cdf` and `poisson_cdf` all inherited it. The same abort was reachable from inputs that are not degenerate at all: `gamma_cdf(2000.0, 2000.0, 1.0)` needs 187 series terms and `chi_squared_cdf(4000.0, 4000.0)` reaches it through `df / 2`, and a chi-squared test on 4000 degrees of freedom is ordinary. Both halves are repaired. `gamma_cdf` and `gamma_sf` now carry the guards `gamma_pdf`, `gamma_inv_cdf` and `weibull_cdf` already use in this module -- NaN for a non-positive `shape` or `scale` and for a NaN argument, the limits 1.0 and 0.0 at x = +inf, 0.0 and 1.0 at x <= 0 -- which agree with SciPy's `gamma` and `chi2` on every row, and they read the standardised argument so a finite `x` whose `x / scale` overflows lands on the same answer. Both incomplete-gamma recursions now spend their 200-iteration budget in chunks of sixteen through an outer driver, costing about 30 frames instead of 200; the iteration sequence, the convergence test and the result are the flat form's bit for bit on every converging input, which is why the 656 existing tests are unchanged. Values change in two places. At degenerate parameters: `gamma_cdf(1.0, 0.0, 1.0)` was `1.0`, `gamma_cdf(1.0, 2.0, -1.0)` was `0.0` and `chi_squared_p_value(5.0, 0.0)` was `0.0`, and all three are now `NaN`. At large shape, where the budget genuinely runs out -- past `shape = 2338` on the series at its worst `x` and past `shape = 47318` on the continued fraction -- the result is `NaN` rather than the unconverged partial sum. Nothing loses a working answer there: exhausting the budget means reaching 200 frames, which aborted the process at every one of those inputs before, and an unconverged sum would be a wrong answer with no signal, where `NaN` is guardable. Accuracy at large shape is a separate, pre-existing limit now documented rather than changed: the f32 series loses about 1.4e-4 relative at `shape = 1000` and 6.5e-4 at 2000. The regression tests in the test lane are value pins rather than detectors, because `chelis test` runs each file on a `chelis-test-worker` thread holding about 500 frames of the same shape and so never reproduced the abort; the detector is `example_gamma_cdf_degenerate_arguments`, which encodes five of the cases as decimal digits and prints `11111.0` under the CI step that evaluates `src/example*.ch`, against exit 134 before this change. The eval-lane limitation itself is drafted at `docs/issue_drafts/chelis_eval_fat_frame_stack_abort.md` and cited at the narrowing site; it is not yet filed upstream. Addresses nautilus#140.
