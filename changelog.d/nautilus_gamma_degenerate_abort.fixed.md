`Nautilus.Distributions.gamma_cdf` and `gamma_sf` validated neither parameter, so a zero or negative `scale` produced a non-finite standardised argument `x / scale`, the continued fraction never satisfied its convergence test, and the recursion spent its whole 200-iteration budget. `chelis eval --file` evaluates on the `main` thread, does no tail-call elimination, and overflows its stack at about 135 frames of a body that size, so the process died with `fatal runtime error: stack overflow` and exit 134 and no diagnostic a Chelis caller could catch; `chi_squared_cdf`, `chi_squared_sf`, `Nautilus.Testing.chi_squared_p_value`, `gamma_inv_cdf` and `poisson_cdf` all inherited it. The same abort was reachable from inputs that are not degenerate at all: `gamma_cdf(2000.0, 2000.0, 1.0)` needs 187 series terms and `chi_squared_cdf(4000.0, 4000.0)` reaches it through `df / 2`, and a chi-squared test on 4000 degrees of freedom is ordinary. Both halves are repaired. `gamma_cdf` and `gamma_sf` now carry the guards `gamma_pdf`, `gamma_inv_cdf` and `weibull_cdf` already use in this module -- NaN for a non-positive `shape` or `scale` and for a NaN argument, the limits 1.0 and 0.0 at x = +inf, 0.0 and 1.0 at x <= 0 -- which agree with SciPy's `gamma` and `chi2` on every enumerated row, and they read the standardised argument so a finite `x` whose `x / scale` overflows lands on the same answer. Both incomplete-gamma recursions now spend their 200-iteration budget in chunks of sixteen through an outer driver, costing about 30 frames instead of 200. The arithmetic is untouched: a red-team pass compared the shipped `gamma_cdf` against a verbatim transcription of the flat recursion over 372 converging points spanning both branches, every residue mod 16 of the series iteration count, and both `1e-30` clamps, and found zero bit differences. A non-converged budget still returns the partial sum, so the only values that change are at degenerate parameters: `gamma_cdf(1.0, 0.0, 1.0)` was `1.0`, `gamma_cdf(1.0, 2.0, -1.0)` was `0.0` and `chi_squared_p_value(5.0, 0.0)` was `0.0`, and all three are now `NaN`, matching SciPy and matching `student_t_cdf`'s existing `df <= 0` rule. An earlier revision of this change also converted a non-converged budget to `NaN`, on the reasoning that every budget-exhausting input had aborted anyway; that reasoning was false outside the `chelis eval` lane, and the rule discarded a 1.5e-4-accurate answer at `shape = 2339` while keeping the 1.6e-3 one at 2338, so it was removed. The series' accuracy limit at large `shape` is long-standing and is now documented rather than changed: relative error at `x = shape` is 1.9e-6 at `shape = 100`, 2.8e-4 at 1000, 6.5e-4 at 2000, 1.6e-3 at 2338 and 16% at 20000, with nothing signalling the transition. The regression tests in the test lane are value pins rather than detectors of the abort, because `chelis test` runs each file on a `chelis-test-worker` thread holding about 500 frames of the same shape; the detector is `example_gamma_cdf_degenerate_arguments`, which encodes five of the cases as decimal digits and prints `11111.0` under the CI step that evaluates `src/example*.ch`, against exit 134 before this change. `betacf_rec` in the same module carries the same budget with a fatter body and is **not** chunked, so `beta_cdf`, `f_cdf`, `student_t_cdf` and `binomial_cdf` can still abort the eval lane; that surface is recorded as uncovered in `docs/UPSTREAM_BUGS.md` rather than claimed fixed. The eval-lane limitation itself is upstream `chelis#2471`. Addresses nautilus#140.
